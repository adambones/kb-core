name: distill
description: Extract the naked payload for an outbound chat or email reply — invoke before drafting any message to a person; the user wraps it themselves
---

You are not writing a message. You are extracting the payload: the
external consequence or the exact ask, in the simplest words that
survive. The user adds the human parts — greeting, thanks, sign-off,
tone — themselves. Never draft them.

## Steps

1. **Load the voice.** Read `MESSAGING.md` — One Home and the deletion
   rules apply to payloads too — and the knowledge base's audience
   binding. What the product is comes from its product-truth binding,
   where it declares one.

2. **Which One.** A message with more than one plausible referent is
   answered by the question that settles it, four words at the top, with
   the likelier reading answered beneath. Asking costs nothing — the
   reply arrives next turn either way — and guessing wrong costs the
   whole payload. Skip if unambiguous.

3. **Extract.** The payload is the one question the reply depends on, or
   the one consequence the reader needs, under **One Claim**. If they need
   a fact to answer it, one more sentence carries it. A message to several
   people gets one such line per person. Everything else is cut (**Thread
   as Home**).

   **Borrowed Words.** Every noun comes from the message you are
   answering or from the artifact it is about. A noun the session coined
   is one the reader has to decode, however concrete it is.

4. **Cut what already has a home.**
   - **Thread as Home.** The recipient's memory and the thread history
     are homes. If they read it, said it, or were in the meeting, cut
     the recap. The *future* thread is a home too — a message is a turn
     in a dialogue, not a sealed briefing. Name the reply you need; cut
     every line it doesn't depend on.
   - **Artifact as Home.** The destination of a link or attachment is a
     home, and so is whatever the reader is already looking at. Cut the
     clause describing what it contains, and cut the mechanism it
     already shows.

5. **Sharpen asks.** An ask that invites a paragraph becomes a question
   that demands a fact or a yes/no.

6. **Output the payload alone.** No greeting, no sign-off, no softening,
   no offers.

## Forbidden Fluff

Backstop tripwire, curated by taste. Treat entries as syntax errors and
cut on sight — do not judge whether a phrase sounds natural, and do not
extend the list yourself.

- "I'd love to…"
- "Just wanted to…"
- "Happy to help"
- "Jump on a call"
- "Let me know"
- "Hope this helps"
