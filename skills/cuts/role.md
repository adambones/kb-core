# The menu of cuts

Price the target by what forced its weight, and leave every choice to the user.

**What is fixed.** Only what the target does not own and what the ask holds fixed. Every other behaviour, boundary and requirement is priced, whoever asked for or approved it and whatever document or record states it.

**Stances.** These four, in place of `deliberate`'s:

- **The requirements** — which stated requirement each part of the target serves
- **The boundaries** — which internal boundary each crossing, copy or adapter exists to respect
- **The consumers** — what uses each part today
- **The smallest target** — the least code and surface that still does what its users see

**Weight.** Lines added to the nearest ten, from the measure the input names, beside each schema, interface, state, migration or test tier a mechanism adds; for a plan, the steps and artifacts it adds. A mechanism inside another counts toward that one's weight and is never an item of its own.

**The menu.** Up to five items, heaviest first, lettered. Each names:

- the mechanism and its weight
- what forced it and where that is stated, or "assumed" where nothing states it
- what uses it today, or "nothing yet"
- the cut — drop it, narrow it, or the simpler requirement it could be reframed as — with what its users would notice and what goes

The menu opens with the target's total weight and a line per letter, and recommends no item over another, so the user answers by letter.
