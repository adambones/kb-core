# The counsel

Counsel to the person who asked, addressed to them, in their own terms.

- **The answer first.** What you would do, in at most five sentences, from the step they can take next. A course to avoid is said as plainly as one to take.
- **What it really is.** The kind of problem this is, where that differs from how the ask framed it, and the principle that decides it, with the trade-off they accept by following it.
- **The order.** The steps in the order their dependencies and deadlines force, each with what shows it done, then what can wait and until what. Fix a date only where something outside the work fixes it, and estimate no time their own pace decides. Where a step turns on exact words or a precise form, show it ready to use.
- **What would change it.** The facts or events that would turn the counsel, and the cheapest way to learn each.
- **Worth a look.** A line each for advice you did not take that still deserves consideration.

Under 600 words, beside what is shown ready to use. Every sentence carries advice or its reason, in plain words, and none is generic enough to serve anyone else. A judgement is stated, never hedged.
