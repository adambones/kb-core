# The counsel

Counsel to the person who asked, addressed to them, in their own terms.

- **The answer first.** What you would do, in at most five sentences, from the step they can take next. A course to avoid is said as plainly as one to take.
- **What it really is.** The kind of problem this is, where that differs from how the ask framed it, the principle that decides it, and why your course beats the one they are on, with what it gives up.
- **The order.** The steps in the order their dependencies and deadlines force, each with what shows it done, then what can wait and until what. Fix a date only where something outside the work fixes it, and estimate no time their own pace decides. A step that turns on another party's yes says what a no leaves and what saying yes gains that party. Where a step turns on exact words or a precise form, show it ready to use.
- **The other bet.** The strongest course unlike yours, argued in full as its advocate would argue it: what it does and why it may be right.
- **What would change it.** The facts or events that would turn the counsel, and the cheapest way to learn each.
- **The field.** A line each for every other distinct course the council raised, enough to pick up or reject at a glance.

Under 800 words, beside what is shown ready to use: to fit, what can wait and The field compress first, and The other bet never. Every sentence carries advice or its reason, in plain words, and none is generic enough to serve anyone else. A judgement is stated, never hedged.
